  Why a third machine doesn't pay yet

  It depends entirely on what the box is:

  - Another WSL2 machine — adds a row to a cross-machine table that isn't the open question. The interesting question is why a box degrades against itself, and a new host with no history can't speak to that.
  - Native Linux, or bare metal, or macOS — now it's interesting, because it tests a hypothesis nothing has tested: is this WSL2-specific? Both petri and NB-3641 run the identical 6.18.33.2-microsoft-standard-WSL2 kernel. "Guest degrades over days, reboot fixes it" is far more
    characteristic of a virtualized guest — host paging, memory ballooning, dynamic memory reclaim — than of bare metal.

  But even then, one arm tells you nothing, because you'd have no baseline for that box. Testing "is it WSL" needs the third machine measured fresh and aged — the same 18-day discipline as E, just relocated.

  What would actually answer it: a drift curve, not more machines

  The unexplained part is that the 09-07 change was a step inside a smooth 18 days, not a drift. Nothing has ever measured petri between fresh and 18 days — you have 0.3 h and 430 h and nothing in between. So:

  Take one 16-minute arm on petri every few days at the same version. That gives you when degradation starts, whether it's a step or a ramp, and whether it reproduces at all. The harness now records uptimeHours and claudeResident in every arm, so the series self-describes.

  And if you can keep Claude Code off petri between captures, that series is arm E — you get the residue answer for free instead of paying 18 days for a single data point. If you can't (likely, it's your main box), run it anyway with normal usage: if a step reappears and lines up
  with a session's start, that's strong evidence for residue; if it appears with no session behind it, uptime accumulates on its own.

  One cheap instrumentation gap worth closing first

  Arm A's whole ambiguity was that claudeResident: {count: 0} can't distinguish "never ran" from "closed it". Every future arm in that series will hit the same wall. A claudeSinceBoot field — inferred from whether anything under /tmp/claude-* has an mtime after boot — would make
  the series self-describing instead of depending on you remembering. It's a small change to capture-interaction-perf.js and a heuristic rather than proof, but it beats recollection, which is what just cost us arm A's precision.
